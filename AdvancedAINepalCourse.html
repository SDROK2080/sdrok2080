AI Fundamentals - Student-Friendly

🧠 What is AI, Really?

▼
Simple Explanation

Imagine teaching a child by showing many examples. After seeing 1000 pictures of cats, a child learns to recognize cats. AI works similarly - we show it many examples, and it learns patterns.

🇳🇵 Nepal Real-Life Example

A Nepali farmer learns over time that tomato prices go up in winter and down after harvest. AI can learn this pattern from historical market data and predict future prices - helping farmers decide when to sell!

Three Types of Learning
  • Supervised Learning: Learn from labeled examples (like learning from a textbook with answers)
  • Unsupervised Learning: Find patterns without labels (like discovering that students naturally group by abilities)
  • Reinforcement Learning: Learn by getting rewards (like learning a game by playing and winning rewards)

🤖 Language Models & ChatGPT

▼
How ChatGPT Works

Imagine someone has read every book, website, and article on the internet. When you ask a question, they remember similar things they've read and make an educated guess about what words should come next.

ChatGPT is trained similarly - it's learned patterns from billions of words, so it can predict what response makes sense for your question.

💡 Important for Nepal

Most LLMs work best in English. They understand Nepali less well because there's less Nepali text on the internet compared to English. This is why building Nepali AI is so important - we need models trained on Nepali language and culture!

LLM Capabilities & Limits
  • Can write essays, answer questions, and have conversations
  • Can sometimes "hallucinate" - make up facts that sound real
  • Doesn't truly "understand" - just predicts word patterns
  • Reflects biases in training data (may not represent all cultures equally)

⚡ Data: The Foundation of AI

▼
Why Data Matters

You could have the best AI algorithm in the world, but if your data is garbage, your AI will be garbage. It's like building a beautiful house on sand - the foundation is everything!

🚨 The Data Challenge in Nepal

Nepal doesn't have much digitized data compared to developed countries. To build great Nepali AI:

  • We need to collect and digitize Nepali text, images, and information
  • We must ensure data represents all Nepali communities fairly
  • Data must be owned and controlled by Nepal, not foreign companies
Data Quality Checklist
  • Accurate: Information must be correct
  • Complete: No missing critical information
  • Representative: Includes diverse examples
  • Fair: Doesn't systematically favor one group over another
  • Secure: Privacy and safety are protected

🤖 Week 1: Advanced AI Agents

▼
What Are AI Agents?

An AI Agent is like hiring an employee who can think, plan, and take actions. It can understand goals, break them into steps, and execute them - even using tools (like calculators or APIs).

Key Agent Concepts
  • Planning: Breaking goals into logical steps
  • Tool Use: Using APIs, calculators, search engines
  • Memory: Remembering conversation history
  • Reasoning: Thinking through problems step-by-step
  • Error Recovery: Fixing mistakes automatically
🇳🇵 Nepal Use Case: Tourist Guide Agent

An AI agent could plan a complete Nepal tour:

1. Ask traveler their budget and interests
2. Check weather in different regions
3. Look up available flights and hotels
4. Plan optimal route (Kathmandu → Pokhara → Himalayas)
5. Get real-time prices and book
6. Handle changes if plans shift

📚 Week 2: RAG - Retrieval-Augmented Generation

▼
What is RAG?

RAG = Smart Database + AI Conversation

Imagine ChatGPT having access to your personal library. It can search your documents to find relevant information, then generate answers based on what it found. This solves the hallucination problem!

💡 Why RAG for Nepal

RAG allows creating AI systems that:

  • Answer questions about Nepali history & culture accurately
  • Reference official government documents
  • Provide information specific to Nepal's context
  • Work in low-resource languages like Nepali
RAG Pipeline Steps
  • Indexing: Convert documents into searchable format (embeddings)
  • Retrieval: Find relevant documents matching user question
  • Generation: Use LLM to answer based on retrieved documents
  • Evaluation: Check if answer is accurate and relevant
🇳🇵 Practical Example: Nepali Constitution Assistant

Users can ask questions about Nepal's constitution in Nepali or English. The system retrieves relevant articles and explains them clearly. Developers, students, and citizens benefit!

⚡ Week 3: RAG Optimization & Evaluation

▼
Making RAG Better

RAG systems can fail in specific ways. We need to:

Detect Hallucinations: When AI makes up information even with documents
Improve Retrieval: Make search more accurate
Balance Speed vs Accuracy: Faster responses vs more correct answers
Optimize Prompts: Better questions = better answers

Optimization Techniques
  • Chunking Strategy: How to split documents
  • Embedding Selection: Which model to convert text to vectors
  • Semantic Search: Understanding meaning, not just keywords
  • Query Rewriting: Improving user questions automatically
  • Latency-Accuracy Tradeoff: Choosing what matters more
🇳🇵 Example: Tourism Information System

Hallucination Problem: User asks "Can I hike Mt. Sagarmatha in February?" System might make up an answer.

Solution: Use RAG with real trekking guides, weather data, and expert information. System retrieves facts instead of guessing.

🎯 Week 4: Fine-Tuning Large Language Models

▼
What is Fine-Tuning?

Like hiring someone trained at Harvard, then teaching them your specific business. ChatGPT is the Harvard grad - fine-tuning teaches it your specific domain or language.

💡 Critical for Nepali AI

Fine-tuning LLMs on Nepali data would create models that:

  • Understand Nepali grammar, slang, and context
  • Generate better Nepali text
  • Cost less to run (smaller models)
  • Keep data within Nepal
When to Fine-Tune
  • ✅ Domain-specific language (medical Nepali, legal Nepali)
  • ✅ Specific writing style or tone
  • ✅ Limited resources in your language
  • ❌ Generic general knowledge (use prompt engineering instead)
  • ❌ When you have small amount of data
Fine-Tuning Options
  • LoRA: Cheap, fast, for small teams
  • Full Fine-tuning: Powerful but expensive
  • Instruction Fine-tuning: Teaching specific tasks
🇳🇵 Use Case: Nepali Poetry Generator

Fine-tune on thousands of Nepali poems to create a model that generates poetry in Nepali style. Much better than English-trained models!

🚀 Week 5: Production & Deployment

▼
Going Live with AI

Building AI in a notebook is fun. Making it work for millions of users 24/7 is hard! Deployment means solving real-world problems like:

💰 How much does it cost?
🔒 Is user data secure?
⚡ Is it fast enough?
📊 Can we handle peak traffic?

Deployment Considerations
  • Cost Optimization: Use cheaper models, cache responses
  • Security: Encrypt data, authenticate users, prevent attacks
  • Scalability: Handle 10x more users without breaking
  • Monitoring: Track errors and performance in real-time
  • Versioning: Safely update models without breaking users
🇳🇵 Cost Reality in Nepal

Challenge: Using APIs like OpenAI's ChatGPT is expensive for Nepal (they charge in USD).

Solution: Run open-source models locally, build regional servers, partner with other South Asian countries to share costs.
