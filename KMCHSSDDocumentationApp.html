Module 1: Course Study Report

1.1 Background

The Humanities and Social Sciences Department of Kapilvastu Multiple Campus offers Bachelor of Arts programs aligned with the Tribhuvan University Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences syllabus. Over time, the department's academic activity has grown to include assignments, research projects, seminars, cultural documentation, alumni tracking, and student feedback. Much of this work was handled through paper forms, individual email messages, and scattered files, which made it difficult to track progress, retrieve records, and communicate consistently across batches.

The KMC-HSSD App was developed as a connected set of web-based tools to bring these functions into one coherent digital environment. It is built on widely available, low-cost infrastructure, including HTML and JavaScript interfaces, Google Forms, Google Sheets, Google Apps Script, and static web hosting, so that it can be maintained by departmental staff without dedicated server infrastructure.

1.2 Purpose

The purpose of the app is threefold. First, it aims to organize academic resources, including the BA program structure, syllabi, academic calendar, and assignment guidelines, in a form students can access easily. Second, it aims to digitize routine academic processes such as registration, assignment preparation, research project submission, and feedback collection. Third, it seeks to promote research, creativity, and cultural documentation among students by providing structured guidance and a record of their work.

1.3 Target Audience

Students are the primary users. BA students at the first through fourth year use the assignment writer, program page, and calendar. Students engaged in research use the research management tools and methodology resources. Students participate in creativity challenges and cultural documentation projects.

Faculty members use the app to monitor submissions, review research progress, and evaluate student work. Supervisors receive assignments of research students and track milestones through the dashboards.

Administrators, including the department head and examination coordinators, use the dashboards and form responses to view aggregate data, schedule activities, and publish announcements. Alumni are a secondary audience, reached through a registration form that helps the department maintain contact and encourages engagement.

1.4 Overall Vision

The long-term vision is a single centralized digital workspace connecting teaching, learning, research, communication, and content management for the department. In this vision, a student enters once and can see their program requirements, submit assignments, register research interests, log participation in seminars and workshops, and share feedback, while faculty and administrators see the same information in organized dashboards. The app is intended to strengthen the department's identity as a center for humanities scholarship in Kapilvastu, where local heritage, including the history of the Shakya region and the cultural traditions of Lumbini and Kapilvastu, is studied and documented through modern research methods.

1.5 Components of the Platform

The KMC-HSSD App comprises several modules. The academic calendar presents the session schedule, pillar-based activity categories (learning, research, innovation, collaboration, and impact), and list and calendar views with research form links. The BA program page organizes Year 1 through Year 4 subjects into compulsory and optional categories, with subject codes, marks, and syllabus references. The BA assignment writer guides students through the department's recommended assignment structure, including cover page, table of contents, introduction, objectives, main discussion, analysis, conclusion, recommendations, and references in APA 7th edition. The research management system tracks student research through a series of Google Forms, linked spreadsheets, and automated scripts, with supervisor assignment and progress logging. The alumni registration form and the student feedback form collect structured information through email-confirmed submissions. The creativity challenge form invites students to submit original ideas and solutions to local problems. The cultural documentary workbook guides students through a sixteen-step documentary project on local culture and identity. The student support portal records individual development activities across research, leadership, career, and student voice categories.

1.6 Technological Infrastructure

The platform uses a deliberately simple stack. Front-end interfaces are single-file HTML applications with embedded CSS and JavaScript, designed to be responsive on mobile devices, since many students access digital resources through smartphones. Data storage uses Google Sheets as the central database, accessed through Google Apps Script web apps that expose simple endpoints for reading and writing records. Browser localStorage serves as a backup so that entries are not lost during connectivity interruptions. Email notifications are handled through FormSubmit or EmailJS services. Hosting is provided through GitHub Pages or a comparable static host. Bilingual English and Nepali interfaces are provided where the audience requires them.

The architecture has notable strengths in cost, portability, and ease of maintenance. It also carries limitations, discussed in Module 4, including dependence on internet access and on Google services, and the absence of formal authentication in the front-end layer.

Module 2: Course Study Guide

This guide explains how to navigate the KMC-HSSD App and complete the most common tasks. It is written for students, with notes for faculty where relevant.

2.1 Getting Started

Open the app link provided by your department or by the department notice board. The home page shows the academic calendar and the main navigation. On a mobile phone, use the menu icon to open the list of modules. On a computer, the navigation appears in a sidebar. Before using any form, check that your internet connection is active, because the status indicator in the portal shows green when the system is connected to the data store, red when it is offline, and blue while loading.

2.2 Student Login and Identification

The app does not require a password for most student modules, because it is designed for a campus environment where identity is confirmed through your roll number and enrollment number. When you first use the student support portal, enter your full name, roll number, enrollment number, email address, and program. Use the same details each time, because the system matches your records through these identifiers. If you change your email address, notify your department coordinator so that your records can be linked correctly.

2.3 Viewing the Academic Calendar

Open the academic calendar and select either the calendar view or the list view. The calendar view shows dates in month format, while the list view shows activities in chronological order. The five pillar tabs group events by purpose: Learn, Research, Innovate, Collaborate, and Create Impact. Select a pillar to see only the activities in that category. Research forms are linked from the calendar, so you can go directly to the form relevant to your current stage.

2.4 Accessing Study Materials and the BA Program

Open the BA program page and select your year, from Year 1 through Year 4. Each year is divided into compulsory and optional subjects. For each subject, the page shows the subject code, full marks, and a link to the syllabus. Where available, previous question papers are linked as well. To check whether an optional subject is available to you, consult your department coordinator, since availability depends on the faculty allocation for each session.

2.5 Preparing and Submitting an Assignment

The BA assignment writer guides you through the recommended structure. Begin by entering your name, roll number, subject, teacher name, and the assignment title. The app then provides sections for each component of the assignment. Write your introduction first, then state your objectives, develop the main discussion with reference to your sources, provide analysis, and conclude with recommendations where appropriate. Format your document in Times New Roman, 12-point type, with 1.5 line spacing, justified text, A4 paper, and one-inch margins. A typical assignment in Humanities and Social Sciences runs between 1,500 and 3,000 words. When your draft is complete, download the document and submit it through the method your subject teacher specifies, whether that is a printed copy, a file submitted by email, or a link to a shared folder.

2.6 Registering a Research Interest

Students who wish to undertake research should begin with the research methodology form. This form asks about your research interest, your preferred methodology, and your familiarity with qualitative and quantitative approaches. Once submitted, you will receive a confirmation email containing a research identification number in the format KMC-HSSD-RES-2026-XXX, which you should keep for all future communication. Your research record is then created in the research management system, and a supervisor may be assigned to you. You can view your progress through the research dashboard using your research ID.

2.7 Submitting a Creativity Challenge Entry

The creativity challenge, titled "One Student, One Idea, One Solution," invites students to propose one original idea addressing a problem in their community, school, or region. To participate, open the challenge form and describe your problem, your idea, the stakeholders affected, and the solution you propose. Include whether you have tested the idea in any way, even informally. Submit the form once. Your entry is recorded in the department's spreadsheet and reviewed by a panel, which will contact selected participants.

2.8 Completing the Cultural Documentary Workbook

The cultural documentary workbook has sixteen steps. Work through them in order, as each step builds on the previous one. Begin by identifying a cultural practice, tradition, or place in your locality. Then record background information, interview at least two informants where possible, collect photographs or video with consent, and write a reflective analysis. Progress is saved in your browser, so complete the workbook on a single device where possible. When finished, submit your documentation through the final step, which sends your entry to the department's collection with the fields for name, roll number, program, category, title, description, municipality, location, and links to your photo and video.

2.9 Recording Activities in the Student Support Portal

The student support portal records activities across nine modules: Home, Student Profile, Academic, Research, Activities, Career, Leadership, Achievement, and Student Voice. To log an activity, select the module and sub-section that matches the experience, for example Activities followed by Seminar, or Research followed by Article. Fill in the title, date, and a short description, and save the entry. Each entry receives a timestamp automatically. The Recent Activities panel on the home page shows your last five entries. Use the Student Voice module to submit suggestions, grievances, or requests for help. These are reviewed by the department coordinator.

2.10 Submitting Student Feedback on Teachers

The student feedback form allows students to evaluate their teachers on fifteen criteria, including conceptual clarity, communication, regularity, use of teaching aids, subject knowledge, critical thinking, curriculum completion, motivation, and alignment with the syllabus. Each criterion is rated on a scale from Average to Outstanding. Complete the student information and course information sections, then rate each criterion honestly. A comments section allows you to add specific observations. The form can be used in English or Nepali. Your feedback is confidential and is used only for improving teaching quality.

2.11 Troubleshooting

If a page does not load, check your internet connection and refresh the page. If the status indicator shows red, your entries are being saved locally and will need to be synchronized when the connection is restored; avoid clearing your browser data until synchronization is confirmed.

If a form shows an error after you click submit, wait a moment and try again, because the data service may be temporarily busy. Do not submit the same form repeatedly, since duplicate entries complicate records. If a link does not open, copy the address into a new browser tab, and if it still fails, notify your department coordinator with a screenshot. If your saved data appears to be missing, check whether you are using the same browser and device as before, since locally stored data is tied to the device. When in doubt, contact the department office rather than attempting repeated fixes.

Module 3: Course Content

This module presents sample study materials, lesson structures, downloadable resources, and assessment components that can be integrated into the KMC-HSSD App. The examples are drawn from humanities and social sciences coursework and are intended as templates for faculty to adapt.

3.1 Lesson Module Structure

Each lesson module follows a consistent structure to support self-paced learning. A module begins with learning outcomes, followed by a brief introduction, core content presented in short sections, an illustrative case drawn from the Nepali context, a set of key terms, a reflection prompt, and a short assessment. Estimated study time is stated at the top of each module.

3.2 Sample Module: Research Methodology in Social Sciences

Learning outcomes for this module are that students will be able to distinguish between qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods research; select an appropriate design for a stated research question; and justify their sampling approach.

The core content explains that qualitative research seeks to understand meaning, experience, and context, while quantitative research measures patterns and tests relationships. A mixed-methods design combines both. As an example, a student studying the role of local festivals in community cohesion in Kapilvastu might use interviews with elders and festival organizers for qualitative insight, and a structured survey of households to measure participation across caste, gender, and age groups.

Key terms include sampling frame, purposive sampling, snowball sampling, triangulation, reliability, and validity.

Reflection prompt: Identify one research question from your own community that could be answered best through qualitative methods, and explain why a survey alone would be insufficient.

Short assessment: Five multiple-choice questions on design selection, followed by one short-answer question asking students to propose a sampling strategy and state its limitations.

3.3 Sample Module: Historical Sources and Method

This module introduces primary and secondary sources in historical research. Learning outcomes include classifying sources, evaluating their reliability, and situating a source within its historical context.

The core content explains that primary sources are contemporary records such as inscriptions, manuscripts, administrative documents, coins, and oral testimony recorded at the time of the events they describe. Secondary sources are later interpretations. Students are introduced to the Ashokan inscriptions at Lumbini as an example of a primary source, and to the accounts of Chinese pilgrims such as Faxian and Xuanzang as travel records that must be read critically for their observations, their assumptions, and their intended audience.

Assessment: A source analysis exercise in which students receive a short extract and answer four questions: who produced it, when and where, for what purpose, and what its limitations are.

3.4 Sample Module: Sociological Perspectives on Community

This module introduces functionalist, conflict, and interpretive perspectives on social institutions. Learning outcomes include explaining each perspective, applying it to a local institution, and comparing the explanatory strengths of each.

A useful applied example is the Dashain festival. A functionalist reading emphasizes how the festival reinforces family bonds, redistributes resources through gifts and tika, and maintains social cohesion across households. A conflict reading examines how the festival may reflect or reproduce inequalities in access to travel, wealth, and ritual roles. An interpretive reading attends to the meanings participants themselves attribute to the festival. Students are asked to write a comparative paragraph applying two of these perspectives.

3.5 Downloadable Resources

The app should provide downloadable resources for each module, including the course syllabus in PDF format, the recommended assignment template in Word format, reading lists with bibliographic details, glossaries of key terms in English and Nepali, and past examination question papers where permitted by the Tribhuvan University Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences. Each resource should carry the department name, the version date, and the responsible faculty member's designation.

3.6 Discussion Topics

Discussion boards or structured forum prompts may be added for each semester. Sample prompts include: To what extent can local heritage be preserved without becoming frozen in time? How should researchers balance academic objectivity with their own community ties? Does the rise of digital media change how cultural traditions are transmitted in rural districts? Students should be required to respond to at least one peer post with a substantive comment that references course material.

3.7 Assessment Components

Assessment within the app includes four components. Short quizzes after each module test recall and comprehension and are auto-graded where the format permits. Written assignments follow the BA assignment format and are submitted through the assignment writer. Research projects are tracked through the research system, with milestones for proposal, literature review, data collection, analysis, and final report. Creativity and documentary submissions are evaluated against published rubrics that assess originality, relevance, evidence, and presentation.

Rubrics should be published in advance, and each assessment should indicate its weighting within the semester's internal evaluation scheme, in accordance with the university's requirements.

Module 4: Project Report

4.1 Introduction

Context

The Humanities and Social Sciences Department at Kapilvastu Multiple Campus serves a student body drawn largely from the local region, many of whom are the first in their families to pursue higher education. The department operates within the framework of the Tribhuvan University Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, which sets the syllabus, examinations, and academic calendar. Within these constraints, the department has room to shape how students engage with the curriculum, how research is supported, and how the institution communicates with its community.

Problem Statement

Before the KMC-HSSD App, departmental processes depended heavily on paper records, individual communication, and personal memory. Students frequently had difficulty locating syllabi, understanding assignment requirements, and knowing which research forms to complete. Faculty had limited visibility into student progress across batches. Administrators received feedback and registration information in forms that were hard to compile. Activities such as seminars, field visits, and cultural documentation were rarely recorded in a way that could be shown to examiners or used for future planning.

Scope

The project covers the design and development of web-based modules for academic information, assignment preparation, research registration and tracking, cultural documentation, alumni registration, student feedback, creativity challenges, and student development logging. It includes the documentation of these modules for students and faculty. It does not cover the replacement of the university's examination system, the integration with the university's central records, or the development of a server-based application with authenticated user accounts, which are identified as future work.

4.2 Objectives

The primary objective is to create a single, accessible digital environment through which students and faculty can perform the core academic functions of the department. A second objective is to reduce duplication and error by collecting data once and storing it in a structured form that can be reviewed and analyzed. A third objective is to encourage research and creative work by making it easier to register interests, track progress, and share outcomes. A fourth objective is to provide the department with reliable data for planning, reporting, and quality assurance, including records of student activities and feedback. A final objective is to achieve these goals at minimal cost and with technical skills that the department can sustain internally.

4.3 Features and System Functionality

The academic calendar presents the session schedule with calendar and list views, categorized by five pillars, and links to research forms. The BA program page provides a structured view of the four-year syllabus, organized by year and by compulsory and optional subjects, with subject codes, marks, and syllabus references. The BA assignment writer guides students through the recommended format and applies the department's formatting standards.

The research management system consists of linked Google Forms, a Google Sheets database, Apps Script automation, and role-based dashboards for students, supervisors, and the department. It assigns each research project a unique identifier, creates a structured folder for each project in Google Drive, tracks progress through defined stages, and records activity in a log. The research methodology form and the creativity challenge form collect initial information and feed the same database.

The cultural documentary workbook guides students through sixteen steps, saves progress in the browser, and submits completed entries to a shared spreadsheet through a web app script. The alumni registration form collects personal, academic, and career information, sends confirmation emails, and stores the submission. The student feedback form captures structured evaluations of teaching on fifteen criteria in both English and Nepali, with email delivery and local backup. The student support portal records activities across nine modules, timestamps entries, and synchronizes them with a Google Sheet, with a status indicator and fallback to local storage when the connection is unavailable.

Across these modules, several functions recur: form validation to reduce incomplete submissions, local storage backup to protect against data loss, responsive layouts for mobile use, and bilingual interfaces where the audience requires them.

4.4 Advantages

For students, the platform provides clear access to program structure, assignment requirements, and research pathways. It reduces the time spent searching for information and allows students to record their achievements in a form that can be shown to employers, scholarship committees, and graduate programs. The cultural documentary and creativity modules encourage students to engage with their own communities as sources of knowledge, which is central to the discipline of humanities and social sciences.

For faculty, the platform centralizes submissions and makes it easier to monitor progress across students. Supervisors can see the status of their assigned research projects without requesting updates through multiple channels. Faculty evaluation of student work becomes more consistent when rubrics are published in the same environment as assignments.

For institutional administration, the platform produces structured data that supports planning, reporting, and quality assurance. Feedback from students and self-evaluations can be analyzed by category to identify areas of strength and concern. Alumni records help the department maintain relationships and identify opportunities for collaboration. The use of low-cost tools means that the department can maintain the system without a large budget, and the modular design allows components to be improved independently.

4.5 Disadvantages and Challenges

The platform has several limitations that the department should acknowledge. The reliance on Google services means that the system depends on the availability of those services and on the continuity of accounts that the department controls. If an account is lost or a script is misconfigured, data access can be interrupted.

The front-end modules store data in the browser as a fallback, which is useful during connectivity problems but means that information on one device may not appear on another. Students who clear their browser data or switch devices may lose unsynchronized entries. The absence of formal user authentication means that the system cannot fully verify identity, and sensitive records such as feedback and self-evaluations depend on the discipline of administrators to protect access.

Internet connectivity is uneven across the district, and some students have limited access to smartphones or computers. Digital literacy also varies, and the platform will require orientation sessions and printed guidance to reach all students equally. There is a risk of over-reliance on digital tools at the expense of library research and direct engagement with primary sources, which the department should guard against.

Maintenance is a further challenge. The system was built incrementally by a small team, and several modules were developed in different sessions with different conventions. Documentation, version control, and a clear process for updating the Apps Script code and front-end files will be needed to keep the platform stable as it grows. Data protection practices, including consent for photographs and interviews in the cultural documentary module and the handling of personal information in forms, must be reviewed against applicable Nepali legal requirements and university policy.

4.6 Conclusion and Future Scope

The KMC-HSSD App demonstrates that a humanities and social sciences department can build meaningful digital infrastructure with modest resources and widely available tools. It brings together academic information, assignment guidance, research management, creative and cultural documentation, alumni relations, feedback, and student development into a set of coherent and accessible modules. Its greatest contribution may be in making the department's work visible, both to students who use it and to the institution that depends on it.

Several upgrades are proposed for future development. The first is the introduction of authenticated student and faculty accounts, which would allow private records to be protected and personalized dashboards to be offered. The second is a consolidated database with clear data-access rules, which would replace the current set of linked sheets with a more robust structure. The third is the development of an analytics layer that produces standard reports on participation, feedback trends, and research progress for the department's annual review. The fourth is an offline-first design that allows students to prepare and save entries without connectivity and synchronize them later. The fifth is the expansion of bilingual content, including Nepali-language study materials for all modules. The sixth is integration with the university's examination and records processes, subject to the university's approval. Finally, the department should establish a governance framework that defines who maintains each module, how changes are approved, and how student data is retained and deleted.

With these developments, the KMC-HSSD App can grow from a collection of useful tools into a durable academic platform that serves the department's students, faculty, and community for years to come.
